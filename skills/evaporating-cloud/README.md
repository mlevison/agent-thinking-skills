# Evaporating Cloud
Part of [Agent Thinking Skills](../../README.md).

Two choices, both reasonable, and they conflict. Often this ends in a win-lose decision or a half-and-half compromise.

The Evaporating Cloud, from Eliyahu Goldratt's Theory of Constraints, looks for a third outcome: both needs met, with the conflict gone rather than split.

## The Problem This Skill Solves
Conflicts get argued at the level of positions:
- **Choosing a new CEO:** promote from within, or hire from outside.
- **Personal budget:** save for a trip, or eat out more often.

The positions are mutually exclusive, so the argument goes nowhere or ends in a compromise.

Behind each position is a need, and both needs serve a shared objective. The links between them rest on assumptions. Find an action that makes one assumption false, and the conflict evaporates, hence the name.

## What You Get
- **Cloud**: five boxes holding the two opposing actions, the need behind each, and the objective both needs serve.
- **Reading**: each side's half of the diagram read aloud as one sentence, from the shared goal, through the need, to the action. By the end, both parties agree the diagram fairly represents the situation.
- **Assumptions**: the "because" behind each arrow, surfaced by questions the skill asks.
- **Injection**: an action that makes one assumption false and meets both needs.
- **Side-effect check**: what the injection could break, asked before we commit.

## When to Use It
- Two people or teams pulling in opposite directions
- A choice where every option costs something important
- A recurring discussion that ends in the same compromise every time
- Being torn between two impulses yourself

**Trigger phrases:** "evaporating cloud", "conflict cloud", "dilemma", "stuck between two options", "either/or", "win-win"

## How It Works
The skill walks through:
1. **Define the conflict:** the opposing wants or actions.
2. **Identify the underlying needs:** what necessary condition does each action satisfy?
3. **Find the shared objective:** what goal do both sides share?
4. **Draw the diagram:** visualise the conflict.
5. **Surface and challenge the assumptions:** which one can be made false?
6. **Test the injection:** what could it break?

The skill doesn't do the thinking for you; instead, it checks the logic and asks the next question.
