# The Universal Intellectual Standards
Nine tests to apply to an answer, not questions to open with. When a reply is thin, work out which standard it fails and ask the matching question.

Name the standard when you use it: *"that's clear, but it isn't precise yet"* teaches the user the tool. Use one at a time.

## Clarity
The gateway. If a statement is unclear you cannot tell whether it is accurate or relevant, so nothing else can be judged.

- Could you say that another way?
- Provide an example. What would that look like on a Tuesday morning?

**Fails like:** "The team needs better alignment." "Quality is slipping."

## Accuracy
Clear and false is still false.

- How do you know that's true?
- How could this be checked?
- Who says so, and what were they in a position to observe?

**Fails like:** "Sixty percent of teams do it this way." "The client won't go for it," from someone who never asked the client.

## Precision
Clear and true but too coarse to act on.

- Can you be more specific?
- How much, how many, by when?
- Which cases exactly: all of them, or the three you're remembering?

**Fails like:** "It takes ages." "Most customers hate it."

## Relevance
True, precise, and about something else.

- That's true, how does that bear on the question at issue?
- What does that change about the answer?

**Fails like:** "We've already spent eight months on it." "We decided this last year."

## Depth
An answer that stays on the surface, skipping the complications and never reaching why the situation exists.

- Are you fixing why this happened, or just cleaning up the mess it made?
- What makes this hard? If it were simple it would be solved.
- What else is pushing or pulling on this beneath the surface?

**Fails like:** Putting a bucket under a leaking ceiling instead of fixing the roof. Telling someone who is overwhelmed to "just get more sleep."

**Systems Thinking:** If the answer is thin because the problem keeps returning, has several causes, or feeds back on itself, the missing depth is structural and these questions will not reach it. Say so, and offer the Systems Thinking skill as a separate exploration. Let the user decide - do not switch approach unasked.

## Breadth
Deep inside one angle, with no other angles explored.

- What would a child ask about this?
- What would [the customer / support / finance / the person you disagree with] say?
- What would someone who reached the opposite conclusion have noticed that you didn't?

**Fails like:** A playground designed by adults who don't have kids.

**Systems Thinking:** This is the closest fit of any standard here. If the angle is narrow because only one role, one function, or one level of detail is in play, the Systems Thinking skill carries a set of perspective shifts built for it - what another role sees, zooming in and out, and which other mental models apply. Offer it as a separate exploration and let the user decide.

## Logic
Parts that are each fine and together don't hold.

- You said X, and now Y. What connects them?
- If nothing connects them, which one gives?
- Does the conclusion claim more than the evidence supports?

**Fails like:** "I don't care what they think," followed by twenty minutes on what they think. "My grandfather smoked until he was ninety, so it can't be that bad."

## Significance
Rigorous work on a question that doesn't matter much.

- Of everything on the table, what matters most?
- Is this the central issue, or a side one that's easier to argue about?
- If you got this exactly right, how much would change?

**Fails like:** Forty minutes on what to call the meeting, none on whether it should happen at all.

## Fairness
Reasoning shaped to reach the answer you wanted.

- Do you have a stake in this coming out a particular way?
- Are you representing their position as they would, or as it's easiest to beat?
- Whose interests are being weighed here? Whose are being sidestepped?

**Fails like:** "So what you're saying is we should do nothing at all." A referee who only looks biased when the call goes against your team.

## Note on the Source
The Foundation for Critical Thinking's page at criticalthinking.org lists seven standards. Paul and Elder's fuller treatment includes Significance and Fairness, kept here because dropping them removes the two tests that catch motivated reasoning.
